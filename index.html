About Me

Nikhil Komalla — Ph.D. Candidate in Computational Materials Science

With over seven years immersed in hydrogen energy and computational catalysis, I am a Ph.D. Candidate in Energy & Mineral Engineering (Minor in Computational Materials) at The Pennsylvania State University. My research bridges quantum-mechanical accuracy and scalable AI — uniting spin-polarized Density Functional Theory (DFT), fine-tuned foundation and magnetic Machine-Learned Interatomic Potentials (MLIPs), and active-learning workflows to accelerate electrocatalyst discovery.

Beyond traditional DFT screening of transition-metal sulfides and complex oxides, I architect GPU-accelerated pipelines for OC25 eSEN and spin-polarized mMACE/MACE fine-tuning, automated multi-MLIP surface adsorption screening (*O, *OH, *OOH, *H, *H2O, and arbitrary adsorbates), explicit microsolvation modeling, and committee-uncertainty-guided DFT validation.
